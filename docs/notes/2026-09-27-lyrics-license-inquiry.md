## Message

Subject: SingBridge licensing scope for phonetic lyrics and synchronized practice

Hello,

We are evaluating lyric licensing for SingBridge, an Android and iOS song-practice app.
The proposed experience combines a visible official YouTube embedded player or lawful user-provided audio with original lyrics and pronunciation-oriented Japanese-to-Hangul or Korean-to-Latin-script output.
We mean phonetic transliteration, not semantic translation.
No commercial lyric integration has been launched.

Could you provide written confirmation and the lowest applicable commercial quote for:

- Original lyric display, line-level synchronization, seeking and repeated practice, including whether you classify this as karaoke.
- Generating and displaying phonetic derivatives alongside originals, including any provider-supplied pronunciation layers.
- Processing lyrics independently supplied by users versus lyrics obtained from your catalog.
- Device caching/offline use, allowed retention, territorial restrictions, attribution/tracking, and deletion or takedown obligations.
- A backend retaining only identifiers/hashes and timing metadata, and whether those derived records carry storage restrictions.
- Optional third-party LLM inference for pronunciation, with no model training; required approval, prompt/log retention, and allowed treatment of generated output.
- API credential architecture and whether direct mobile-client access is permitted.
- Contract minimums, term, usage pricing, covered repertoire, and excluded rights or territories.

Please quote illustrative 1,000 and 10,000 monthly-active-user scenarios and identify how quotes differ for South Korea, Japan, and the United States.
These are comparison scenarios, not committed launch markets or usage forecasts.
Please distinguish rights included in the standard API terms from those requiring a separate agreement, and state whether future account sync or user sharing would require additional rights.

Thank you,
SingBridge project
